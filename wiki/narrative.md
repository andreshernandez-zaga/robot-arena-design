# Narrative

> Status: **Open.** This page collects themes, possibilities, and notes, not a fixed plot.

## Themes we're drawn to

- **Rebuilding from fragments.** Knowledge was lost, and what remains is partial.
- **Isolation and reconnection.** Separate civilizations, each with its own approach to robotics.
- **Contact through competition.** The arena as a means of communication and exchange.
- **Evolution.** Designs improve by crossing with others.
- **Machines without AI.** Rudimentary robots that are not fully autonomous.

## Seed premise (one option)

- The distant future. Humanity has spread across galaxies, and its civilizations have drifted apart.
- A virus that targets AI wipes out every AI system, along with most of humanity's knowledge stored on those systems.
- Isolated pockets of humans keep enough knowledge to build basic robots that make decisions in a rudimentary way, without real AI.
- The civilizations find a way to communicate, but only through **simulated arenas** where their robots fight.
- Shared goal: develop the best robot squads to defend humanity against whatever caused the virus, which may eventually threaten humans directly.

## Why the premise fits the mechanics

- **Arenas as the only channel** means crossing blueprints *is* the knowledge exchange between civilizations.
- **Non-autonomous robots** explain why the player controls them so closely.
- **Lost and scarce knowledge** justifies the limited memory slots and limited crossings.
- **Copying rather than stealing** makes sense: blueprints are information, not physical robots.

## Tensions worth exploring

- **Irony:** after an AI catastrophe, humanity is once again building increasingly capable machines. Is it repeating its mistake?
- Are the other civilizations allies, rivals, or both?

## Alternatives to keep open

- A purpose for the arenas other than defending humanity.
- A setting without galactic isolation.
- Different reasons robotics has to be rebuilt.

## Undecided

- Who is the player in this world?
- What the arenas are for.
- What caused the virus, and whether it is still out there.
