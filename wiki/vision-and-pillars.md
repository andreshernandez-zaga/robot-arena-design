# Vision & Pillars

[Wiki home](README.md) · [Narrative](narrative.md) →

## The fantasy

You are a robot designer. Combat is your test bench. Over your lifetime as a player, you build up a small, personal set of designs shaped by every opponent you have faced.

## Pillars

1. **Select by phenotype, inherit genotype.**
   You judge robots by how they look and how they performed. Their blueprint stays hidden until you commit.

2. **Form follows blueprint.**
   A robot's physical appearance comes from its design. Heavy materials look bulky and light ones look lean. Your designs are something you can *see*.

3. **Every match is information.**
   Turns play out visibly: moves, damage, effects. Afterward there is a light, non-overbearing way to study what happened and decide which designs interest you.

4. **Evolution through crossing.**
   Blueprints are structured (likely as trees, inspired by genetic programming) and can be crossed. Nothing guarantees a better result. Offspring can be better, worse, or just different.

5. **Scarcity forces identity.**
   Crossings are limited and blueprint memory is limited. Players keep making trade-offs, and those choices define them as designers.

6. **Knowledge is copied, not stolen.**
   Acquiring a blueprint copies it. The opponent loses nothing.

7. **Relaxed, asynchronous play.**
   Plan your turn, submit it, put the phone away, and come back later. No 10-minute sessions staring at a screen.

## What makes it attractive

- Crossing blueprints is the differentiator. Squad tactics, action points, and async turns are proven but common. Evolving designs through the people you fight is not.
- The mechanics and the story reinforce each other: scarce knowledge, contact through arenas, rebuilding robotics from fragments.
- Players can grow attached to designs that are visibly *theirs*.
- Experimenting with genetic programming is part of what makes this fun to build.
